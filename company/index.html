01The company

Who they are, in five lines.

Acclym (formerly Agritask)

  • Israeli "crop supply intelligence" company that helps food and beverage firms manage environmental impact and supply resilience.[2]
  • Acquired by the Israeli agtech company CropX Technologies in September 2025.[3]
  • Works across more than 6 million hectares in 39 countries.[2]
  • Raised US$42M in venture funding.[3] It has a track record of about 15 years.[3]

Still to check: the founding year, headcount and head office. We could not confirm them on the company's site.

02The product

One product: the crop supply intelligence platform.

The problem it solves

Large buyers cannot see what happens on the many farms that supply them. Acclym collects field data into one platform, so a company can track crop status and risk and report on sustainability. Today the people who pay for it are the global food companies that buy crops.[2]

Proof that it works

Named customers include Nestlé, AB InBev, General Mills, McCain Foods and Ritter Sport.[2][3] It covers 39 countries.[2] We found no tea customer and no Sri Lankan presence, so our plan is a new market for Acclym.

03Why Sri Lanka

The problem exists in Sri Lanka today.

The problem in Sri Lanka

Tea earned US$1.51bn in 2025, the second-largest merchandise export.[4] Cyclone Ditwah (Nov 2025) caused about US$4.1bn of damage, around 4% of GDP.[5] Plantation groups need to see weather risk estate by estate.

The first client

Hayleys Plantations runs three plantation companies with 55 estates. Together they produce 5.4% of the country's tea and 6.7% of its rubber.[6] A large group like this can also be our local partner, which is the usual way in.[1]

Competition

Not researched yet. To check: local agri-data startups, the Tea Research Institute's tools, and global platforms already working with Sri Lankan estates.

04How we chose

Four tests. Acclym passes all four.

4/4 pre-flight checks done

The five candidates from the course brief

  • Acclym (ex-Agritask): chosen. The need is live today, there is one clear client, and that client earns dollars from exports.
  • Netafim / Phytech: strong product, but hardware spending is hard in an economy short of capital.
  • ThetaRay: fits the Port City plan, but bank compliance sales are long and regulated.
  • BioCatch: proven, but the bank market is smaller and payment in dollars is less certain.
  • Optibus: would mean selling to government (public transport), the slowest cycle.
