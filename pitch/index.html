Tea pays the bills. Weather is now the risk.

Speaker notes · 0:20–0:50

Tea earned one and a half billion dollars last year. It is the export the world knows as "Ceylon". Then Cyclone Ditwah did damage worth about four percent of GDP. Planters now need to see each estate's risk before the buyer asks. And the country wants digital, data-driven tools. That is our opening.

We scored all five options from the brief. 1:20 · The company

Acclym turns every field into data a buyer trusts.

Who they are

  • Israeli "crop supply intelligence" company, formerly Agritask.[3]
  • Acquired by Israel's CropX Technologies in September 2025.[4]
  • Over 6M hectares in 39 countries.[3]
  • Customers include Nestlé, AB InBev, General Mills.[3]
  • US$42M raised in venture funding.[4]

What a tea estate gets

  • One map of every estate, field by field: crop status, weather and risk.
  • Earlier warning of yield loss after floods or drought.
  • Traceability and sustainability records that export buyers ask for.
  • Managers and field officers share one view, so head office sees what the estate sees.

The use case for tea is our proposal. Acclym's site does not list tea customers.

Speaker notes · 1:20–1:50

Acclym, formerly Agritask, is an Israeli platform that tells big food companies what is happening on the farms that supply them. Nestlé and AB InBev use it. Since 2025 it is part of CropX, so it now has soil sensors as well. For a tea estate, that means one map, early warning, and the traceability data buyers ask for.

Speaker notes · 1:50–2:20

Our first client is Hayleys Plantations: three plantation companies and 55 estates across several climate zones. That is enough variety to prove the tool in one season. And because Hayleys is a large conglomerate, it can also be our local partner, which is the usual way into this market.

2:20 · Culture is the strategy

English works. Trust decides.

Four tendencies we plan around. These are starting points we check with real people, not rules about a nation.

Israeli default

"Let's get to the point. Is it a yes?"

VS
What we will do in Colombo

Tea first, small talk, listen. Leave a short brief they can forward internally. Follow up with patience.

Speaker notes · 2:20–3:10

Here the Israeli instinct works against us. The diplomat told us: get an introduction, dress to show respect, phrase questions with care, and expect a longer turnaround. So we arrive through a warm intro, greet the most senior person first, and treat "we will try" as a maybe to explore. We never push for a yes in the room.

3:10 · The road to the meeting

From warm intro to a one-season pilot.

Weeks 1–4 the introduction

The Embassy, our mentor and the Israel Export Institute help us find a contact at Hayleys Plantations.

Weeks 5–8 the pre-read

We send a short, simple brief they can forward inside the organisation. This makes it easy to build consensus internally.

Week 10 the visit to Colombo

We avoid Poya days and April. Ayubowan, both hands for cards, accept the tea. Our CEO meets their head of plantations.

Season 1 the pilot

Two estates in two climate zones, priced in USD, with success measures agreed in writing.

Speaker notes · 3:10–3:40

Our roadmap is slow on purpose. First the introduction, then a short pre-read they can share inside the organisation, then the visit to Colombo, timed around Poya days. We ask for something small: a one-season pilot on two estates. Small enough to approve, big enough to prove the value.

3:40 · Practice and close
Illustrative scenario

End of the first meeting. The head of plantations smiles and says: "This is very interesting. We will try to look at a pilot." What do we say?

Our pick: the second option, then the third. Find the decision path, then follow up in writing, with patience.

Sip the tea. Hear the maybe. Prove it on two estates.

Speaker notes · 3:40–4:10

A last test. If they say "we will try", we do not ask for a signature. We ask who else should be involved, and we follow up in writing. Our message in one line: sip the tea, hear the maybe, and prove it on two estates. Thank you. Bohoma sthuthi.
